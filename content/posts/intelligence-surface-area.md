Until now, companies were constrained by the people they could hire. This is no longer the case. Frontier AI models already show superhuman performance across most knowledge-work tasks, at costs that are already trivial and are continuing to fall.

The bottleneck is no longer about AI being smart enough, but instead the ability of a business to orchestrate its workflows so that this intelligence can be used. By Intelligence Surface Area, I mean every decision point in the organization where data flows in, choices are made, and actions are taken. The more of these points that are exposed, the more leverage AI provides. 

Most companies today are not set up for this. Workflows and data sit in siloed tools and spreadsheets, while critical context is sent over chat and email. That structure means that AI is usable only as a chatbot on the side, rather than working directly on the problems. This is what I see most companies doing today. 

It feels a bit like asking a consultant for advice once in a while (small surface area) versus having teammates who know your work inside-out, directly researching, testing, and solving problems alongside you (large surface area).

Claude is trying to solve this with Cowork, and all the AI labs are trying to also solve it through computer use. But I still believe that isn't the ideal solution to *really* scale intelligence.

Companies should be actively figuring out how to get all data into systems that are accessible via APIs, how to document workflows, and how to capture the decision making of its employees (that's where the most important 'tacit' knowledge of the company is). Any tool that requires access only via a user interface is not something that can be scaled.

The challenge is to make every decision point in the org legible to AI. The companies that win won't be those with the smartest AI, but those that give it the most surface area to work on.
