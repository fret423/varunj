One of the major differentiators that businesses will have in the coming years will be their capability to scale the amount of intelligence applied to problems across the board.

As we think through and improve internal processes at my company, what I see happening is that the way work is done needs to be rethought from scratch. This is because it isn't just about mapping a specific task from a human to AI, but instead to rethink it in terms of what is possible now that was never possible before.

Previously high level (human) intelligence was very expensive and rare - an intelligence problem. At the same time, it is difficult to motivate that level of people to work on what may seem like trivial areas - a scale problem. 

<strong> Levels of a problem </strong>

Problems ideally need intelligence applied at different 'levels'. For example, very simplistically: (1) Strategic Planner, which guides the vision and design. (2) Day-to-day execution. (3) Reviewer of the work. Often problems have one owner, and it was up to that owner to switch between those modes. What I've seen of businesses is that the first (strategy) and third (review and reflection) mode most often get ignored completely, in favour of heads-down execution. This leads to a suboptimal process being executed repeatedly.  

When you can spin up multiple 'intelligences' to work on any problem, this no longer needs to be the case. You can have each of these levels worked on as needed. And there can be many more levels as well. 

Peter Thiel is famed to have a policy to allow each team member to only work on a single problem. I believe the reason behind this is that the singular focus will force the problem to be solved sooner. The ability to have narrow, specialized focus on each level of each problem in parallel can completely change the speed and quality of running a business.
